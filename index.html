About

I am an AI engineer with a Master's degree in Language Technology from the University of Gothenburg and six years of experience across data analysis, software engineering, business intelligence and applied AI. My work focuses on natural language processing, large language models, and multimodal human–AI interaction.

Most recently I designed a hybrid NLP and machine translation pipeline in collaboration with Volvo Trucks, combining terminology-aware rule-based methods with LLM refinement. For my Master's thesis I built a real-time multimodal conversational system integrating LLMs, vision-language models and the Furhat social robot, and conducted user studies on human–AI experience design.

My path into AI came through civil engineering, data analysis and technical sales. That background shapes how I approach research: I care about rigour, but also about systems that work in practice, with real users and real constraints.

Combining subjects that seem opposite

I have always been drawn to the space where apparently opposite disciplines meet. Art and mathematics, meditation and robotics, engineering and storytelling — these are not opposites to me, they are different lenses on the same problem. My Master's thesis is perhaps the clearest example: I built a system where a social robot, powered by large language models and vision-language models, guides a human through a meditation experience. A meditation practice is quiet, inward, contemplative. A robot is engineered, computational, outward. Putting them together forced me to think carefully about what "human–AI interaction" actually means when the AI is not just answering questions but shaping an experience.

That thesis combined four things that rarely sit in the same room: conversational AI, real-time computer vision, human–robot interaction, and empirical user research. I designed the dialogue architecture, built the full pipeline, ran the studies, and analysed both the quantitative and qualitative results. What I learned is that the technical work is only half the problem — the other half is understanding how people actually respond to a system, and designing for that reality rather than for the model's benchmark scores.

This is also why I care about optimising processes, not just building models. An AI system that works in a notebook is not the same as one that works in a factory, a clinic, or a workshop. At Volvo Trucks, I spent as much time refining the data pipeline, evaluating edge cases, and aligning with the standardisation team as I did on the model itself. At Mainsim, I designed ETL pipelines and BI dashboards that had to be reliable, maintainable, and useful to people who were not data scientists. At Smurfit Westrock, I worked on pricing and sales analytics where the value came not from the sophistication of the method but from the clarity of the decision it supported.

I believe the best AI work is done by people who can move between disciplines, question assumptions, and quietly improve the process around them. My motto is: inspire and get inspired. I am at my best when I am learning something I do not yet understand, and when I am turning that understanding into something that works for real people.

  • Human–AI interaction — conversational agents, social robotics, and empirical evaluation of how people experience AI systems.
  • Applied machine learning for industry — data pipelines, MLOps, and the translation of research prototypes into production systems.
